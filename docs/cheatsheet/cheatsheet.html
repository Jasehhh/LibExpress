At a glance 9 TABLES · BCNF

Copies, not titles

A loan is for one physical book_copy, a weak entity keyed by (book_id, copy_no).

Nothing derived is stored

Copy counts, loan status, payment status and member totals are worked out when read.

Withdraw, don't delete

Lowering a book's copy count sets withdrawn_at, so loan history stays.

Rules in transactions

Borrowing limits are checked inside a transaction, with row locks so two requests can't both pass.

Likely questions

Why is book_copy a weak entity?

copy_no restarts at 1 for every book, so it only identifies a copy together with book_id. A copy can't exist without its book.

Why is a loan an entity, not a relationship?

The same member can borrow the same copy many times, and a fine belongs to one specific loan, so each loan needs its own id.

What is the cardinality of incurs?

One-to-one. A fine has exactly one loan (1..1, total); a loan has at most one fine (0..1). The unique loan_id enforces it.

Why not store total_copies or loan status?

They depend on other rows or on today's date, not on the key alone. Storing them could go stale, so they're computed when read.

Then why is fine.amount stored?

It's what was charged at the rate in force that day. Changing the rate later mustn't rewrite old fines.

Is the schema in BCNF?

Yes. In every table, the left side of each non-trivial FD is a candidate key: id, or an alternate key like isbn, email or loan_id.

Why doesn't fine link to member?

The member is reachable through the loan. A second link would repeat that fact and could disagree with it.

What stops two staff lending the last copy?

loan.create locks the book row (FOR UPDATE). If anything slips past, the partial unique index rejects a second open loan on that copy.

Why no FK on activity_log.entity_id?

It's a historical record. The row it names may have been deleted since, and the log entry must survive that.

What happens when the copy count goes down?

Copies on the shelf are withdrawn, highest number first. If not enough are on the shelf, the update fails.

Why raw SQL in lockRow?

Prisma has no SELECT … FOR UPDATE. Everything else goes through Prisma's query API.

Why RESTRICT on activity_log.admin_id?

The log no longer keeps a copy of the admin's email, so deleting the admin would lose who made the change.
